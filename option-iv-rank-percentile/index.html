Option Implied Volatility Rank (IVR) Guide

What is IV Rank (IVR)?

IV Rank measures current IV relative to its 52-week low and high. An IV Rank of 80 means current IV is at 80% of its annual range—signifying extremely high option premiums suitable for option selling strategies.

IV Rank vs IV Percentile

IV Rank: Compares current IV to absolute min/max endpoints.
IV Percentile: Measures the percentage of trading days in the past year where IV was lower than current IV.

💡 Pro Hack: Sell Options When IVR > 50

When IVR is above 50, option premiums contain significant fear markup. Option sellers gain a statistical edge from theta decay and volatility crush.

⚡ Buy Options When IVR < 20

When IVR is under 20, options are cheap. Use net debit strategies like Long Calls, Long Puts, or Debit Spreads to minimize time decay loss.

🛡️ Earnings Volatility Crush

IV Rank spikes right before quarterly earnings. Selling credit spreads just before earnings captures rapid premium deflation.

⚠️ 3 Common IV Rank Mistakes

  • • Buying Options at High IVR (>70): Buying calls/puts during high IVR subjects your position to severe volatility crush even if direction is correct.
  • • Ignoring Delta & Strike Selection: High IVR provides a premium edge, but bad strike selection can still result in losses.
  • • Confusing Absolute IV with IV Rank: An absolute IV of 25% might be high for Nifty (IVR 80) but extremely low for a volatile stock like Tata Motors (IVR 10). Always look at IVR!

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Implied Volatility Rank (IVR)?
IV Rank (IVR) measures where the current Implied Volatility (IV) of an option stands relative to its 52-week (1-year) high and low IV values on a scale from 0 to 100.
What is the formula for IV Rank (IVR)?
IV Rank (IVR) = [(Current IV - 52-Week Low IV) / (52-Week High IV - 52-Week Low IV)] × 100.
What is the difference between IV Rank and IV Percentile?
IV Rank compares current IV against the absolute 52-week min and max levels. IV Percentile measures the percentage of days in the past 252 trading days where the IV was lower than the current IV.
Why is IV Rank crucial for option sellers?
When IV Rank is high (>50), option premiums are rich and expensive, giving option sellers a statistical advantage due to volatility crush. When IV Rank is low (<20), option buying is generally preferred.
What is considered a good IV Rank for selling options?
An IV Rank of 50 or higher indicates that options are overpriced compared to historical levels, making strategies like Iron Condors, Covered Calls, and Short Straddles highly profitable.
How does Volatility Crush impact options trading?
Volatility Crush occurs when uncertainty drops rapidly (e.g. immediately after earnings or Union Budget announcements), causing option premiums to deflate rapidly even if the spot price moves very little.
Can IV Rank be applied to Nifty and BankNifty options?
Yes! IV Rank is widely used for Nifty, BankNifty, Finnifty, and high-beta F&O stocks to select optimal trading strategies.
What trading strategies should I use when IV Rank is below 20?
When IV Rank is below 20, option premiums are cheap. Traders prefer net debit strategies such as Long Calls, Long Puts, Calendar Spreads, or Debit Spreads.
