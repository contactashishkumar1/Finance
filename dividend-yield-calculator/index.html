Dividend Investing & Passive Income Guide

Current Yield vs Yield on Cost

Current Dividend Yield = (Annual Dividend / Current Share Price) × 100

Yield on Cost (YOC) = (Annual Dividend / Average Buy Price) × 100. Over long horizons, your YOC grows exponentially as companies raise dividend payouts.

Taxation of Dividends in India

Dividends are added to your total income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.

Companies deduct 10% TDS under Section 194 if dividend paid to a resident individual exceeds ₹5,000 in a financial year.

💡 Pro Hack: Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

Reinvesting your annual dividend payouts to purchase additional shares creates a compounding snowball effect. Over 15–20 years, dividend reinvestment contributes up to 40% of total equity returns.

⚡ Importance of Yield on Cost

If you bought a stock at ₹100 paying ₹3 dividend (3% yield) and over 10 years the company increases dividend to ₹15/share, your Yield on Cost becomes 15% annually on your initial investment.

🛡️ High-Yield PSU Advantage

Indian Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) like Coal India, REC, PFC, and IOC consistently yield 5%–8% annually, offering fixed-income-like cash flow with equity upside.

⚠️ The High Yield Dividend Trap Warning

  • • Falling Stock Price Illusion: A stock whose price drops from ₹100 to ₹20 while paying a ₹2 dividend looks like a "10% yield", but reflects a business in severe financial distress.
  • • Debt-Funded Dividends: Always check if a company funds its dividend from free cash flows (FCF) rather than taking on debt to pay dividends.
  • • Buying Right on Ex-Dividend Date: Stock prices drop by the exact dividend amount on the ex-dividend date, negating instant arbitrage profits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Dividend Yield?
Dividend Yield is a financial ratio that expresses annual dividend payout as a percentage of the stock's current market price. Formula: Dividend Yield % = (Annual Dividend Per Share / Current Stock Price) × 100.
What is Yield on Cost vs Current Dividend Yield?
Current Dividend Yield compares dividend to today's market price. Yield on Cost compares dividend to your original purchase price. As companies increase dividends over time, your Yield on Cost can grow to 10%-20%+ on your initial investment.
Are dividends taxable in India?
Yes. Dividends received from Indian companies are taxable in the hands of the investor at their applicable income tax slab rate. TDS @ 10% is deducted by companies if dividend payout exceeds ₹5,000 in a financial year.
What is a good dividend yield for Indian stocks?
In India, a dividend yield above 3% to 5% is considered high. PSU stocks (e.g. Coal India, REC, PFC, IOC) often offer higher dividend yields compared to private growth stocks.
What is Dividend Payout Ratio?
Dividend Payout Ratio measures the percentage of net profit a company distributes to shareholders as dividends. A sustainable payout ratio is typically between 30% and 60%.
How does reinvesting dividends accelerate wealth compounding?
Reinvesting dividends to buy more shares (Dividend Reinvestment) creates a compounding snowball effect where future dividend payouts grow exponentially due to a higher share count.
What is the Ex-Dividend Date?
To be eligible to receive a dividend, you must buy the stock before its Ex-Dividend Date (since T+1 settlement requires shares to be in your demat account on the Record Date).
Do REITs pay higher dividends than regular stocks?
Yes! Indian REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) are legally required to distribute at least 90% of their net distributable cash flows to unitholders, often yielding 6% to 7% annually.
