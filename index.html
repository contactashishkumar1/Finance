How does the SIP Calculator compute future mutual fund returns? ▾

Our SIP Calculator applies the compound interest annuity formula:

M = P × [((1 + i)^n - 1) / i] × (1 + i)

Where P is your monthly installment, i is the monthly periodic return, and n is the number of months. You can also model annual top-ups using our Step-Up SIP Calculator.

How can I compare Old vs New Tax Regime for FY 2026-27? ▾

You can use our dedicated Income Tax Calculator and Tax Regime Breakeven Tool.

The calculator compares the New Tax Regime (with ₹75,000 standard deduction and Section 87A full rebate up to ₹12 Lakhs taxable income) against the Old Tax Regime with Chapter VI-A deductions (Section 80C, 80D, 80CCD, HRA exemptions) to show exactly which regime saves you more money.

How does an EMI calculator work for Home, Car, and Personal loans? ▾

Our Home Loan EMI Calculator uses the banking standard reducing balance method formula:

E = P × r × (1 + r)^n / [((1 + r)^n) - 1]

It generates your exact monthly repayment, interest vs principal breakdown, and a full monthly/annual amortization schedule.

What is the difference between In-Hand Salary and CTC? ▾

CTC (Cost to Company) is the complete expense a company incurs to employ you (basic pay, HRA, employer EPF, gratuity, and medical insurance). In-Hand Salary is the net amount deposited in your bank account after mandatory salary deductions like Employee EPF (12%), Professional Tax, and Income Tax (TDS). Calculate your take-home pay instantly using our In-Hand Salary Calculator.

What is the difference between CAGR and XIRR for calculating returns? ▾

CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate) measures annualized point-to-point return for a single one-time lumpsum investment. XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return) computes annualized returns when there are multiple periodic cash flows at irregular dates (such as monthly SIP installments and periodic partial withdrawals).
