Understanding Section 194-IB (TDS on Rent)

To bring high-value rental transactions under the tax net, the government introduced Section 194-IB. Under this section, individuals or HUFs who pay a monthly rent of more than ₹50,000 are required to deduct Tax at Source (TDS) and deposit it with the government.

TDS Rates & Penalties

Scenario TDS Rate Action Required
Rent is ≤ ₹50,000/month 0% No TDS deduction required.
Rent > ₹50,000/month & PAN Provided 2% Deduct 2% from total annual rent in the final month.
Rent > ₹50,000/month & PAN NOT Provided 20% Deduct 20% (capped at 1 month's rent). Landlord loses out heavily.

When and How to Deduct TDS

Unlike business TDS which is deducted monthly, Section 194-IB is designed to be easy for common individuals. You only need to deduct TDS once in a financial year.

  • Deduction Time: You must deduct the entire year's TDS amount from the rent paid for the month of March (the last month of the financial year).
  • Vacating Property: If you vacate the property in the middle of the year (e.g., November), you must deduct the TDS from the rent paid in November (the last month of your tenancy).
  • No TAN Required: You do not need a Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN). You can file Form 26QC using just your PAN and your landlord's PAN.

Example Calculation

Suppose you pay a rent of ₹60,000 per month for 12 months.
Total Rent = ₹7,20,000.
TDS @ 2% = ₹14,400.
For 11 months (April to Feb), you pay the landlord the full ₹60,000.
In March, you deduct the ₹14,400 TDS. The rent you pay the landlord for March will be: ₹60,000 - ₹14,400 = ₹45,600.
You then deposit the ₹14,400 to the government via Form 26QC before April 30.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is required to deduct TDS under Section 194-IB?
Any individual or HUF who is NOT liable to tax audit, and pays a monthly rent exceeding ₹50,000, must deduct TDS under Section 194-IB.
What is the rate of TDS under Section 194-IB?
The TDS rate is 2% (reduced from 5% under Budget 2024/2026 rules) if the landlord has provided a PAN. If the landlord does not provide a PAN, the TDS rate is 20%.
When should I deduct TDS under 194-IB?
TDS is to be deducted only once in a Financial Year. It is deducted from the rent paid in the last month of the financial year (March), or in the last month of tenancy if the property is vacated during the year.
What if the TDS amount is more than the last month's rent?
If the landlord does not provide a PAN, the TDS is calculated at 20%. However, the law specifies that the TDS amount cannot exceed the rent payable for the last month. The calculator automatically handles this cap.
Do I need a TAN to deduct TDS under 194-IB?
No, you do not need a TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) to deduct TDS under Section 194-IB. You can use your PAN and the landlord's PAN to file Form 26QC.
How do I deposit the TDS to the government?
You must deposit the deducted TDS within 30 days from the end of the month in which the deduction is made, by filling out Form 26QC on the Income Tax e-filing portal.
What happens if I fail to deduct or deposit TDS?
Failure to deduct TDS attracts interest at 1% per month. Failure to deposit deducted TDS attracts interest at 1.5% per month. There is also a late filing fee of ₹200 per day under Section 234E.
Do I need to issue a TDS certificate to the landlord?
Yes, after filing Form 26QC and paying the tax, you must download Form 16C from the TRACES portal and provide it to your landlord within 15 days as proof of tax deduction.
