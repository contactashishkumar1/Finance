US to Mexico Money Transfer Guide

Everything you need to know about transferring USD to MXN. Discover the fastest, cheapest, and most secure ways to navigate the largest remittance corridor in the Americas.

Introduction to the US-Mexico Corridor

The US to Mexico remittance corridor is an economic powerhouse, responsible for moving tens of billions of dollars southward every year. Driven by millions of Mexican nationals and Mexican-Americans supporting family members, it is one of the most highly developed and competitive money transfer routes in the world.

Unlike corridors dominated entirely by digital bank-to-bank transfers, the US-Mexico route is heavily defined by its physical infrastructure. While direct bank deposits into Mexican accounts via the SPEI network are growing rapidly, cash pickup remains king. A vast network of pharmacies, supermarkets, and convenience stores—most notably OXXO and Elektra—act as the primary payout points for millions of recipients.

This comprehensive guide breaks down the mechanics of sending money from the US to Mexico, detailing the balance between digital funding and cash delivery, exchange rate margins, and the strategies you need to maximize the pesos received by your beneficiary.

Why This Corridor is Unique

The US to Mexico corridor stands out for several structural reasons that dictate how transfers operate:

  • Cash is Still King: Despite the growth of FinTech in Latin America, a massive percentage of recipients in rural or semi-urban Mexico remain unbanked or prefer dealing in cash. Transfer providers must integrate with local retail networks to be competitive.
  • Hyper-Local Retail Networks: Providers like Xoom, Remitly, and Western Union don't just partner with banks; they partner with retailers like OXXO, Bodega Aurrera, Walmart Mexico, and Elektra. This turns virtually every corner store into a remittance payout location.
  • SPEI Infrastructure: For those who are banked, Mexico's central bank operates SPEI (Sistema de Pagos Electrónicos Interbancarios), a highly efficient real-time gross settlement system that allows for instant bank deposits 24/7.

Popular Transfer Methods

Digital to Cash Pickup

The most popular hybrid method. You fund the transfer digitally in the US via an app like Remitly or Xoom, and the recipient walks into a local Mexican retailer (like OXXO) minutes later to collect physical pesos.

Digital to Bank (SPEI)

Companies like Wise and Instarem excel here. You send USD from your US bank, and it is deposited directly into the recipient's Mexican bank account using their 18-digit CLABE number. Highly cost-effective.

Physical Agent to Cash

Traditional methods using Western Union, MoneyGram, or local US remittance storefronts. You hand over cash in the US, and the recipient collects cash in Mexico. Reliable, but often carries higher fees.

Traditional Bank Wires

Sending a wire directly from a US bank to a Mexican bank. While highly secure for massive sums (e.g., buying real estate), it is the most expensive method for everyday family support due to flat outbound wire fees.

Typical Transfer Costs

Transferring USD to MXN incurs two types of costs: the upfront transfer fee and the exchange rate margin.

Digital Providers: You can expect total effective costs (fees + markup) ranging from 1.0% to 2.5% of the transfer amount. Many providers advertise "$0 fee" transfers to Mexico but apply a heavy markup on the peso exchange rate.

Cash Pickup Premium: Note that providers often charge slightly higher fees or offer a worse exchange rate if you select "Cash Pickup" compared to "Bank Deposit," as maintaining retail networks costs them more money.

Exchange Rate Considerations

The USD/MXN exchange rate is highly liquid and frequently traded, but consumer providers heavily monetize the spread on this corridor.

For example, a provider might quote you 16.50 MXN for 1 USD when the actual Google mid-market rate is 17.00 MXN. That difference of 0.50 MXN per dollar is the markup. On a $500 transfer, that hidden markup costs you 250 MXN in lost value.

Pro Tip: Providers targeting the US-Mexico corridor are notorious for offering promotional "first-time" exchange rates that are artificially high, only to drop you to a much poorer rate on your second transfer. Always use our Exchange Rate Markup Calculator before every single transaction.

Transfer Speed Expectations

Speed in the US to Mexico corridor is dictated by how you fund the transfer in the US.

  • Debit/Credit Card Funding: If you fund via a US debit card, providers can instantly release the funds to the Mexican retail network for cash pickup, or instantly deposit them via SPEI.
  • ACH Bank Debit: The cheapest funding method, but the legacy US ACH system takes 1 to 3 business days to clear. Providers usually wait for the ACH to clear before releasing funds in Mexico, delaying the transfer.
  • Cash at Agent: Handing over cash at a Western Union in the US results in funds being available for pickup in Mexico within minutes.

Payment Methods (US)

You must fund your transfer using US-based financial instruments or cash:

  • ACH Direct Debit: Slowest (1-3 days) but usually free.
  • Debit Card: Very fast, moderate fees.
  • Credit Card: Fast, but carries high provider fees and potential cash advance fees from your card issuer. Not recommended.
  • Cash at Agent: Reliable for unbanked senders in the US, but carries higher fees.

Receiving Methods (Mexico)

Mexico offers two primary receiving avenues:

  • Cash Pickup: The most popular. Available at OXXO, Elektra, BanCoppel, Walmart, and pharmacies. Requires the transfer PIN and a valid INE/passport.
  • Direct Bank Deposit (SPEI): Requires the recipient's 18-digit CLABE number. Funds arrive directly into their checking or savings account.

Regulatory Environment

Both the US and Mexico heavily scrutinize this corridor due to its massive volume and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) concerns.

In the US: Providers are regulated by FinCEN. Any transaction over $10,000 must be reported to the IRS via a Currency Transaction Report (CTR). Frequent, slightly smaller transfers (structuring) will trigger algorithmic red flags and account suspensions.

In Mexico: The government places strict limits on the amount of physical USD cash that can be exchanged or deposited at banks. This is why remittances are settled in MXN. Furthermore, retail locations (like OXXO) have strict daily payout limits per person (e.g., usually capped around 10,000 to 20,000 pesos) to prevent money laundering via retail networks.

Tips to Reduce Costs

  • Push for Bank Deposits: Encourage your recipient to open a basic Mexican bank account if possible. Providers almost always offer better exchange rates and lower fees for CLABE deposits compared to cash pickups.
  • Avoid Credit Cards: The combination of provider percentage fees and cash advance charges will destroy your value.
  • Shop Around Monthly: The US-Mexico corridor sees constant promotional pricing wars. Don't be blindly loyal to one app.

Common Mistakes

  • Name Mismatches: When sending for cash pickup, the name you enter in the app must match the recipient's INE (voter ID) exactly, including both paternal and maternal last names (apellidos). Even a small typo will cause the retailer to refuse the payout.
  • Ignoring Payout Limits: Sending $2,000 USD to a small rural pharmacy for cash pickup. They likely will not have enough physical cash on hand or will hit their regulatory payout limits. Send large amounts to banks.

Frequently Asked Questions (US to Mexico)

What is the best way to send money to Mexico for cash pickup?
Digital providers like Remitly, Xoom, and traditional giants like Western Union are excellent for cash pickup. They partner with massive retail networks like OXXO, Elektra, and Bodega Aurrera, allowing your recipient to collect cash minutes after you send it.
How long does it take for money to reach Mexico from the US?
If you fund your transfer with a US debit card, cash pickup and direct bank deposits (via SPEI) are often available in minutes. If you use a US bank account (ACH) to fund the transfer, it typically takes 1 to 3 business days.
What details do I need to send money to a Mexican bank account?
To send money directly to a bank account in Mexico, you need the recipient's full name, the name of their bank, and their 18-digit CLABE (Clave Bancaria Estandarizada) number.
What does the recipient need to pick up cash in Mexico?
The recipient will need the exact transaction reference number (MTCN or tracking pin) and a valid, government-issued photo ID (like an INE credential or passport) that perfectly matches the name you entered on the transfer.
Are there limits on cash pickup amounts at OXXO or Elektra?
Yes. Retail locations in Mexico impose limits on how much cash can be picked up per transaction and per day, often capping around 10,000 to 20,000 MXN depending on the specific retailer and local regulations.
Why shouldn't I use my US bank to wire money to Mexico?
Traditional US banks charge high outbound wire fees (often $35-$50) and typically offer poor USD/MXN exchange rates. Additionally, wire transfers cannot be used for cash pickup, which is the preferred receiving method for many in Mexico.
What is Xoom and is it good for Mexico?
Xoom is a PayPal service heavily marketed for the US-Mexico corridor. It is very fast and deeply integrated with Mexican retail networks. However, its exchange rate markup is often higher than competitors like Remitly or Wise, making it more expensive for larger transfers.
Does the recipient in Mexico pay a fee to receive the money?
No, the recipient does not pay a fee to collect the cash or receive the bank deposit. All fees and exchange rate markups are paid by the sender in the US upfront.
