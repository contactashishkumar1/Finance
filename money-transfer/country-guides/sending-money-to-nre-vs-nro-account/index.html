Sending Money to NRE vs NRO Account Guide

Should you transfer foreign earnings to an NRE or NRO bank account in India? Understand FEMA rules, tax-free interest, 30% TDS, and the $1M repatriation limit.

Introduction to the USD-INR Corridor

When Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) remit money to India, deciding between an NRE (Non-Resident External) account and an NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account is one of the most critical financial and tax decisions they face.

Under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), holding a standard resident savings account while residing abroad is illegal. Choosing the right account type determines whether your interest income is 100% tax-free in India and whether your principal capital can be transferred back overseas without complex regulatory clearance.

Why This Corridor is Unique

The USD to INR remittance corridor stands out for several structural reasons:

  • Foreign Earnings vs Domestic Indian Income: NRE accounts are designated exclusively for foreign-earned income remitted from abroad; NRO accounts manage local Indian income (rent, dividends, asset sales).
  • Complete Tax Exemption on NRE Interest: Interest earned on NRE savings and Fixed Deposits is 100% exempt from Indian income tax under Section 10(4)(ii); NRO interest is subject to 30% TDS plus cess.
  • Repatriability & The $1 Million USD Annual Quota: NRE funds are freely and fully repatriable overseas at any time; NRO funds can be repatriated up to $1,000,000 USD per financial year subject to Form 15CA/CB tax clearance.

Popular Transfer Methods

Digital Remittance Specialists

Companies like Wise and Instarem dominate this route by utilizing local clearing bank networks, offering benchmark mid-market rates and low upfront fees.

Traditional SWIFT Bank Wires

Direct international wire transfers between retail commercial banks. High security for large sums, but generally the most expensive due to wire fees and FX markups.

Instant Account / UPI Rail Transfers

Pushing funds directly to recipient bank accounts or virtual payment addresses (e.g. UPI / GCash) for instant settlement in seconds.

Cash Pickup Agent Networks

Networks like Western Union allow physical cash collection at agent counters across the destination country for unbanked recipients.

Typical Transfer Costs

Transferring USD to INR incurs two potential costs: upfront service fees and hidden exchange rate spreads.

Digital Providers: Remitting foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR, AED) into an NRE account via digital transfer specialists (Wise, Remitly, Instarem) converts at wholesale mid-market rates with transparent fees under 0.5%.

Traditional Retail Banks: Sending foreign currency wires directly via traditional retail banks into NRE/NRO accounts incurs correspondent bank deductions ($25) and bank conversion spreads of 1.5% - 2.5%.

Transfer Speed Expectations

Transfer settlement speed depends primarily on how you fund the transaction in USD:

  • Digital Remittance to NRE: Credits into Indian NRE accounts within minutes to 24 hours via IMPS/NEFT rails.
  • SWIFT International Bank Wire: Takes 2 to 4 business days to clear and credit into NRE/NRO accounts.
  • NRO to NRE Account Transfer: Requires 2 to 4 business days for Chartered Accountant 15CB certification and bank verification.

Payment Methods (USD)

Primary options for funding transfers in USD:

  • Digital Remittance App (Wise/Instarem): Direct conversion and deposit into NRE account with mid-market benchmark rates.
  • International Bank Wire (SWIFT TT): Traditional foreign currency telegraphic transfer from overseas bank accounts.
  • Foreign Currency Cheque / Demand Draft: Paper instrument deposited at Indian bank branches; takes 15-21 days to clear.

Receiving Methods (INR)

How beneficiaries receive INR funds:

  • NRE Savings / Fixed Deposit Account: 100% tax-free interest, freely repatriable principal and interest.
  • NRO Savings / Fixed Deposit Account: Manages domestic rent and dividends; interest subject to 30% TDS.
  • FCNR(B) Deposit Account: Hold foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR) directly in India with zero currency risk.

Regulatory Environment

Both sending and receiving jurisdictions enforce strict financial regulations to prevent money laundering and ensure taxation compliance.

In Origin Jurisdiction: Under US IRS rules, US-based NRIs must report foreign accounts on FBAR (FinCEN 114) if total foreign balances exceed $10,000 USD.

In Destination Jurisdiction: Under FEMA regulations, NRIs are legally required to re-designate resident bank accounts to NRO upon acquiring non-resident status.

Tips to Reduce Costs

  • Park Foreign Savings in NRE Fixed Deposits: NRE FDs offer attractive interest rates (7.0%+ in INR) with zero Indian tax deduction.
  • Submit TRC & Form 10F for NRO DTAA Relief: Submitting a Tax Residency Certificate reduces NRO TDS from 30% to 10%-15% under DTAA treaties.
  • Move Eligible NRO Funds to NRE Annually: Utilize the $1M annual quota to transfer tax-paid NRO funds to NRE for future unrestricted liquidity.

Common Mistakes

  • Retaining Resident Savings Accounts: Failing to convert resident accounts to NRO upon becoming an NRI violates FEMA regulations.
  • Depositing Domestic Cash into NRE: Resident Indians cannot deposit domestic cash into an NRE account; deposits must originate from foreign sources.

Step-by-Step NRI Guide: Allocating Inward Capital Between NRE & NRO

To optimize your tax efficiency and repatriation flexibility as a Non-Resident Indian, follow this banking allocation strategy:

  1. Determine Fund Origin: If the funds originate from foreign employment earnings or overseas business profits, remit 100% of the funds into your **NRE (Non-Resident External) Account** in INR. NRE balances earn completely tax-free interest in India and are 100% freely repatriable abroad at any time.
  2. Reserve NRO for Domestic Indian Incomes: Use your **NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account** strictly to receive domestic Indian earnings—such as rental income from Indian property, ancestral dividends, director fees, or local property sale proceeds.
  3. Deposit Tax-Exempt Family Gifts into Resident Accounts: If remitting money to support resident Indian parents or spouse, transfer funds directly into their personal resident savings account. Remittances to linear relatives are 100% tax-exempt in India under Section 56(2).
  4. Repatriating from NRO under $1M Quota: If you need to move accumulated funds from your NRO account back overseas or into your NRE account, obtain Form 15CB certified by a practicing Chartered Accountant and submit Form 15CA online.
  5. Maintain FEMA Compliance Status: Ensure you do not hold standard resident savings accounts in India once your tax residency transitions to NRI status. Convert all existing resident accounts to NRO status as mandated by the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I transfer money from an NRO account to an NRE account?
Yes. Under RBI regulations, funds can be transferred from NRO to NRE within the $1 Million USD annual limit, provided you submit Form 15CA and a Chartered Accountant-certified Form 15CB.
Is interest on an NRE Fixed Deposit taxable in the country where I reside?
While NRE interest is 100% tax-free in India, it may be taxable in your country of residence (e.g. US IRS taxes worldwide income). Consult local tax treaties (DTAA) for specific rules.
Can a resident Indian transfer money into my NRE account?
No. Resident Indians cannot deposit domestic INR into your NRE account (except for legitimate gifts/loans under the LRS scheme). Resident income must be deposited into an NRO account.
What is Form 15CA and Form 15CB for NRO repatriation?
Form 15CA is an online remittance undertaking filed by the remitter, and Form 15CB is a certificate issued by a practicing Chartered Accountant confirming that appropriate taxes have been paid on the repatriated funds.
What happens to my resident bank account when I become an NRI?
Under FEMA regulations, you are legally required to re-designate your resident savings bank accounts into an NRO account upon acquiring Non-Resident Indian status.
Which account should I use to invest in Indian Mutual Funds and Stocks?
You can invest through both: investments made via NRE are fully repatriable, whereas investments funded from NRO are subject to capital gains tax and standard NRO repatriation limits.
Are there limits on sending foreign money into an NRE account?
No. There are zero limits on the amount of foreign earnings you can remit into your NRE account from abroad.
Can I use an NRE debit card outside India?
Yes. NRE international debit cards can be used globally, drawing directly from your INR balance at the bank's prevailing international exchange rate.
