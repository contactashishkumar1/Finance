📊 Cost Anatomy

The 3 Components of International Transfer Cost

An international money transfer fee is rarely just the advertised upfront charge. The true cost is a combination of three distinct fee layers:

1. Upfront Fee Visible

The flat or percentage service charge displayed before confirming the transfer ($0 to $45).

2. FX Rate Spread Hidden

The margin added to the wholesale mid-market rate. Often represents 70%+ of total bank charges.

3. Intermediary SWIFT Deducted

Third-party routing banks deduct $15 to $30 from the principal during traditional wire transit.

True Cost = Upfront Fee + (Send Amount × FX Markup %) + SWIFT Fees Digital Specialists Save ~75%
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What are SWIFT intermediary fees?
When you send a traditional international bank wire, the money often passes through 1 to 3 correspondent banks. These banks charge a fee (typically $10 to $30) for routing your money, which is deducted in transit.
What does SHA mean?
SHA stands for 'Shared'. You pay your sending bank's outbound fee upfront. The intermediary and receiving bank fees are deducted from the principal amount before it arrives.
What does OUR mean?
OUR means the sender pays all fees. You pay the sending fee, plus an estimated charge to cover intermediary and receiving fees upfront. The recipient should receive the exact principal amount.
What does BEN mean?
BEN stands for 'Beneficiary'. The sender pays no upfront fees; instead, all sending, intermediary, and receiving fees are deducted directly from the transfer amount before it is deposited.
Why are intermediary and receiving fees just 'estimates'?
Because the SWIFT network is decentralized, your sending bank rarely knows exactly which route the money will take or what the specific receiving bank charges for incoming foreign wires.
Does this calculation include the exchange rate markup?
No. Bank wires also apply an exchange rate margin (usually 2% to 5%) which acts as a massive hidden cost on top of the fixed wire fees calculated here.
Why are bank wires so expensive?
They rely on decades-old correspondent banking infrastructure requiring manual checks, multiple hops, and high overhead costs compared to modern digital treasury networks used by specialist providers.
How can I avoid SWIFT fees?
Use specialized money transfer providers (like Wise, Remitly, or XE). They use local bank accounts in various countries to settle transfers domestically, bypassing the expensive SWIFT network entirely.
