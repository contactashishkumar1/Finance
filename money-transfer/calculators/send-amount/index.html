Understanding the Math

Understanding how transfer providers compute this specific metric allows you to dissect their fee structures and uncover hidden costs.

The Formula:
Required Send Amount = (Desired Received Amount / Exchange Rate) + Total Fees

Step-by-Step Guide

  • Identify your variables: Gather the precise fee numbers, exchange rates, and principal amounts from your provider's quote.
  • Input the figures: Enter these values into the calculator above. Be sure to use the correct formatting for percentages and exchange rates.
  • Analyze the output: The calculator instantly applies the formula to determine the exact metric. Use this output to compare multiple providers objectively.
Application

Practical Example

Imagine you are sending a remittance of $1,000. You compare two providers to find the best deal. Provider A charges a flat fee of $10, while Provider B charges a 2% variable fee. By using our mathematical formula, you can clearly evaluate the financial impact.

For Provider A, the fee is a simple $10. For Provider B, 2% of $1,000 equals $20. In this scenario, despite Provider A charging a prominent upfront fixed fee, it is mathematically cheaper than Provider B's percentage-based approach.

As the transfer size grows, the difference becomes starker. This is why utilizing objective calculators is crucial before confirming an international payment.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Send Amount Calculator most useful?
It is highly useful when you must pay an exact foreign invoice (e.g., paying a freelancer exactly 1,000 EUR, or settling a precise university tuition fee). It ensures the recipient isn't short-changed by conversion math.
Why can't I just guess the send amount?
Because exchange rates involve multiple decimal places and fees complicate the math, guessing often results in sending slightly too much or too little. If you send too little, you may incur another full set of fees just to send a tiny top-up balance.
Does this calculation ensure the specific payout?
If you are using a provider with locked exchange rates and zero intermediary fees, yes. However, if using traditional bank wires (SWIFT), intermediary banks may still deduct fees in transit unless you specify an 'OUR' charge type.
How are fees added to this calculation?
First, the calculator divides the target foreign amount by the exchange rate to determine the base amount needed. Then, it adds any upfront flat or percentage fees on top of that base amount to give you the total funding requirement.
Can I lock in the exchange rate to ensure accuracy?
Yes, many modern digital transfer providers allow you to lock in an exchange rate for 24 to 72 hours while you initiate the bank transfer to fund your payment.
What happens if the exchange rate changes before I send the funds?
If your rate isn't locked, market fluctuations mean you might need to send a slightly different amount than originally calculated. Always refresh the rate immediately before transferring.
Do receiving banks charge fees on exact payouts?
Sometimes. Even if you calculate the exact amount to send, the recipient's bank might charge an 'incoming wire fee'. You may need to ask the recipient if their bank charges such fees and add that to the target amount.
Is this formula the same for all currencies?
Yes. The mathematical logic applies universally, regardless of the currency pairs involved. You just need to ensure the exchange rate is entered in the correct format (e.g., how much foreign currency 1 unit of your local currency buys).
