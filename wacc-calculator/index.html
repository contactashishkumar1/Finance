Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) Formula

  • Formula: WACC = (E/V * Ke) + (D/V * Kd * (1 - Tax Rate)).
  • Cost of Equity (Ke via CAPM): Ke = Risk-Free Rate (Rf ~7.0%) + Beta * Equity Risk Premium (ERP ~5.5%).
  • After-Tax Cost of Debt: Debt interest is tax-deductible under Income Tax Act; effective Kd = Pre-tax Interest * (1 - 25.17% Tax Rate).

Optimal Capital Structure & Hurdle Rates

  • Hurdle Rate for Capital Expenditure: WACC represents the minimum return a company's projects must generate to avoid destroying shareholder value.
  • Leverage Effect: Adding low-cost debt initially lowers WACC, but excessive leverage increases financial distress risk.

Corporate Valuation Benchmark

In India, large-cap blue-chip corporate WACC ranges between 10.5% and 12.5%. Use this WACC range when discounting cash flows in DCF valuation models.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is WACC?
WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) represents a company's average cost of capital from all sources, including common stock, preferred stock, and debt.
What is the WACC formula?
Formula: WACC = (E/V * Ke) + (D/V * Kd * (1 - T)), where E = Market Value of Equity, D = Market Value of Debt, V = E + D, Ke = Cost of Equity, Kd = Cost of Debt, and T = Tax Rate.
How is Cost of Equity (Ke) calculated using CAPM?
Ke = Risk-Free Rate (Rf) + Beta * Equity Risk Premium (ERP). In India, the 10-year G-Sec yield (~7.0%) is standard for Rf.
Why is tax shield included in Cost of Debt?
Interest paid on corporate debt is tax-deductible under Income Tax Act. The effective cost of debt is reduced by multiplying by (1 - Tax Rate).
What is a good WACC benchmark for Indian companies?
In India, typical corporate WACC ranges between 10.0% and 13.5%, depending on debt ratios and stock volatility (Beta).
How does WACC impact hurdle rate for new capital projects?
WACC serves as the minimum hurdle rate. A new investment project must generate a Return on Capital (ROCE) higher than WACC to create economic value (EVA).
Should market value or book value weights be used?
Financial theory mandates using Market Value weights for both equity (market cap) and debt rather than historical balance sheet book values.
How does raising more debt affect WACC?
Debt is cheaper than equity due to tax deduction. Increasing debt initially lowers WACC, but excessive leverage increases financial distress risk, driving up both Ke and Kd.
