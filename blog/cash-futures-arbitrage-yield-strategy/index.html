Cash-Futures Arbitrage: Earning Risk-Free Yields on Stock Futures Basis

Want market-neutral risk-free returns higher than bank FDs? Discover how cash-futures arbitrage captures the basis spread between spot and futures prices.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Cash-Futures Arbitrage involves simultaneously buying stock in the cash (spot) market and selling the identical quantity in the futures market when futures trade at a premium (contango), locking in a risk-free return.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Market-Neutral: Completely immune to stock market crashes or rallies.
  • Lock-in Yield: Returns are locked upon order execution regardless of final expiry price.
  • Roll Spread Yield: Rolling over near-month short futures can generate annual yields of 7%-10%.
  • Equity Tax Advantage: Classified as equity for tax purposes when held in mutual funds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cash-Futures Arbitrage 100% risk-free?

Yes, provided both legs are executed simultaneously and held until expiry when cash and futures prices converge to exact parity.

What is Contango vs Backwardation?

Contango occurs when futures trade at a premium to spot (enabling positive arbitrage). Backwardation is when futures trade at a discount to spot.

Do I need full cash to buy the spot stock?

Yes. You must purchase the exact lot quantity in the cash delivery segment to fully hedge your short futures contract.

What is rollover spread in arbitrage?

Rollover spread is the premium earned by closing the expiring futures contract and selling the next month's futures contract.

Why do futures trade at a premium to spot?

Futures trade at a premium due to the 'cost of carry' (prevailing interest rate cost to hold the underlying asset until expiry).
