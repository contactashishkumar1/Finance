US Stock Investing for Indians: FX Conversion Markup, Dividend Withholding & LTCG Math

Investing in US stocks like Apple, Nvidia, or Tesla from India? Learn how FX conversion markups, 25% US dividend withholding tax, and 12.5% LTCG tax impact net returns.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

US stock investing involves 3 friction layers: 1) FX transfer & conversion fees (2%-3%), 2) 25% US dividend withholding tax (creditable in India via FTC), and 3) 12.5% Indian LTCG tax on equity gains held over 24 months.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Holding Period for LTCG: 24 months for unlisted/foreign shares (taxed at 12.5% without indexation).
  • US Dividend Tax: 25% flat withholding tax deducted at source by US broker under India-US DTAA.
  • Foreign Tax Credit (FTC): Claim US dividend tax paid against Indian tax liability using Form 67.
  • LRS Remittance Limit: Up to $250,000 USD per individual per financial year.

Statutory Legal Provisions & Regulatory Framework

Navigating US Stock Investing for Indians in India requires a thorough understanding of the relevant Income Tax Act sections and regulatory guidelines established by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT):

  • Statutory Tax Exemption Provisions: Specific sections of the Income Tax Act govern allowances, deductions, and capital gain exemptions. Ensuring full compliance through proper documentation prevents tax audit notices and interest penalties under Section 234B/234C.
  • Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime Choice: Under Section 115BAC, individual taxpayers can evaluate tax liabilities under both regimes annually. While the New Tax Regime offers lower slab rates and a higher rebate under Section 87A, the Old Tax Regime remains advantageous for individuals claiming substantial HRA, Section 80C, 80D, and Home Loan interest deductions under Section 24(b).
  • Form 26AS & Annual Information Statement (AIS): Tax authorities automatically cross-verify salary Form 16, bank interest deposits, stock market capital gains, and high-value financial transactions against your AIS record.

US Dividend Withholding Tax & Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA)

When investing in US stocks (Apple, Microsoft, S&P 500 ETFs) from India:

  • 25% US Dividend Withholding Tax: Under the US-India DTAA treaty, the US Internal Revenue Service (IRS) automatically deducts a 25% withholding tax on all dividends paid by US companies to Indian resident investors (via Form W-8BEN).
  • Claiming Foreign Tax Credit in India (Form 67): You can claim the 25% US tax withheld as a credit against your Indian income tax liability by filing Form 67 before submitting your ITR-2 return, completely eliminating double taxation!
  • 24-Month Holding Period for LTCG: Foreign shares are treated as unlisted equities in India. The threshold for Long-Term Capital Gains is 24 months, taxed at 12.5% LTCG without indexation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians pay capital gains tax in the United States?

No! Under the US-India Tax Treaty, non-resident foreign investors pay ZERO capital gains tax to the US government. Capital gains are taxed exclusively in India.

How are dividends from US stocks taxed for Indian residents?

The US government deducts a flat 25% withholding tax at source on dividend payouts. You report dividend income in your Indian tax return and claim Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) using Form 67 to prevent double taxation.

What is the holding period for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on US stocks?

Foreign stocks must be held for more than 24 months to qualify for LTCG (taxed at 12.5%). If held for 24 months or less, gains are treated as STCG and taxed at your income slab rate.

Is Schedule FA declaration mandatory in ITR for US stock holdings?

YES! Under the Black Money Act, any Indian resident holding foreign assets (including 1 fraction of a US stock) MUST disclose them in Schedule FA of their ITR, regardless of income.

How does USD/INR currency movement affect US stock returns?

If the US Dollar appreciates against the Rupee (e.g., from ₹80 to ₹85), your investment value in Rupees increases automatically, boosting your total return.
