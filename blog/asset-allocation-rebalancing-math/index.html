Portfolio Rebalancing Strategy: When & How to Rebalance Equity vs Debt

A bull market can quietly shift a conservative 60/40 portfolio into a high-risk 80/20 equity allocation. Learn how disciplined portfolio rebalancing protects your wealth from sudden market crashes.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Portfolio rebalancing realigns your asset weighting back to its target risk capacity by selling outperforming assets and purchasing underperforming ones. It is a risk-control mechanism designed to enforce 'Buy Low, Sell High' discipline.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Risk Control Focus: Rebalancing is designed to control portfolio volatility, not boost annual returns.
  • 5% Corridor Rule: Rebalance whenever an asset class drifts by ±5% or ±10% from your baseline target.
  • Tax-Efficient Rebalancing: Direct new monthly SIP inflows into underweight assets to avoid triggering capital gains tax.
  • Pre-Retirement Shift: Shift equity growth units into conservative debt buckets 3 years prior to retirement.

The Danger of Portfolio Drift in Bull Markets

Imagine setting up a balanced portfolio in January with ₹60 Lakh in Equity Mutual Funds (60%) and ₹40 Lakh in Debt Funds (40%). During a strong 2-year bull market rally, your equity portfolio surges by 50% to ₹90 Lakh, while your debt funds grow modestly to ₹44 Lakh.

Your total portfolio value is now ₹1.34 Crore. However, equity now represents 67% of your total wealth, while debt has shrunk to 33%. Without realizing it, your portfolio has drifted into a significantly higher risk bracket.

If a sudden 30% market crash hits at this moment, your un-rebalanced portfolio will lose ₹27 Lakh in equity value. If you had rebalanced back to 60/40 prior to the crash, your loss would have been limited to ₹24 Lakh—saving ₹3 Lakh of hard-earned capital.

Two Core Rebalancing Strategies: Calendar vs Threshold

Investors typically choose between two mathematical approaches to portfolio rebalancing:

  • Calendar Rebalancing: You review your asset allocation on a fixed date once a year (for example, every April 1st after tax year close). It is simple, highly disciplined, and requires minimal ongoing monitoring.
  • Threshold (Tolerance Band) Rebalancing: You set a corridor limit (typically ±5% or ±10%). If your target equity allocation is 60%, you rebalance ONLY when equity weighting drops below 55% or rises above 65%. This method captures extreme market panic and euphoria mid-year.

How to Rebalance Without Paying Taxes

Selling appreciated equity units held for less than 12 months incurs 20% STCG tax, while units held over 12 months incur 12.5% LTCG tax above the annual ₹1.25 Lakh exemption threshold. To avoid triggering capital gains tax during rebalancing:

  • Redirect Fresh Monthly SIPs: Pause SIPs into the overperforming asset class and direct 100% of new monthly SIP cash into the underperforming asset class until target weights are restored.
  • Deploy Annual Bonuses: Use annual salary bonuses, tax refunds, or dividend payouts to buy units in the underweight asset class.
  • Utilize Tax Harvesting: Redeem up to ₹1.25 Lakh of long-term equity capital gains each financial year tax-free to shift capital into debt funds.

Rebalancing Triggers: Calendar Rebalancing vs Threshold Rebalancing

Rebalancing forces you to systematically buy low and sell high without emotional interference:

  • Threshold Rebalancing (5/25 Rule): Rebalance only when an asset class deviates by 5% absolute (e.g. equity target 60% rises to 65%) or 25% relative to its target allocation. This minimizes unnecessary transaction costs and capital gains taxes.
  • Cash Flow Rebalancing (Zero Tax Friction): Instead of selling appreciated equity units and triggering 12.5% LTCG tax, redirect fresh monthly SIPs and annual bonuses into under-allocated debt or gold funds until target weights are restored.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I rebalance my portfolio?

Reviewing your portfolio once a year (Calendar method) or whenever an asset class drifts by more than ±5% to ±10% (Threshold method) is optimal for retail investors.

Does portfolio rebalancing increase returns?

Not necessarily. Rebalancing is a risk mitigation tool designed to keep portfolio volatility within your risk capacity, preventing panic selling during crashes.

Why should I sell winning equity funds to buy debt?

Rebalancing enforces the golden rule: 'Buy Low, Sell High'. It locks in equity profits after rallies and buys cheap debt/equity units before reversals.

What is the 5% corridor rule?

If your target equity allocation is 60%, you rebalance only if equity weighting moves below 55% or above 65%.

Should retirees rebalance differently?

Yes. Retirees should maintain a 3-bucket strategy and rebalance Bucket 1 (Liquid Cash) annually from Bucket 3 (Equity) after strong market years.
