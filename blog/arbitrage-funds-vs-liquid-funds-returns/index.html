Arbitrage Funds vs Liquid Funds: Returns, Risk & Exit Loads Compared

Looking for a safe, high-yield alternative to traditional bank savings accounts? Compare Arbitrage Mutual Funds with Liquid Debt Funds to optimize your short-term cash parking strategy.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Arbitrage Funds exploit price differentials between cash and futures markets, offering equity-like tax treatment (20% STCG / 12.5% LTCG). Liquid Funds invest in short-term money market instruments maturing in 91 days, delivering superior capital stability taxed at your income slab rate.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Tax Efficiency: Arbitrage Funds are taxed as equity funds (≥65% equity derivative holding), making them superior for investors in 30% tax bracket.
  • Return Mechanics: Arbitrage returns thrive during volatile, high-spread bull markets; Liquid Funds yield steady interest from T-Bills and Commercial Papers.
  • Exit Load Window: Liquid Funds offer zero exit load after 7 days; Arbitrage Funds typically charge 0.25% exit load if redeemed within 15 to 30 days.
  • Market Neutrality: Both funds carry near-zero directional stock market crash risk.

🎯 What You'll Learn

How cash-futures basis spreads create risk-free arbitrage yields and how liquid fund NAVs accrue daily interest.

How Arbitrage Funds Make Money in Contango Markets

When stock markets are active or bullish, stock futures usually trade at a premium compared to their current cash (spot) price. This market condition is known as contango. Arbitrage fund managers exploit this price gap by simultaneously buying shares in the cash delivery market and shorting (selling) the identical quantity in the futures market.

Because cash and futures prices are legally mandated to converge to exact parity on monthly options expiry day, the fund locks in a risk-free profit margin regardless of whether the market rallies, crashes, or trades sideways. On expiry day, the manager simply unwinds both legs or rolls over the futures contract to the next month to capture fresh spreads.

Because SEBI mandates that Arbitrage Funds hold at least 65% of their total assets in equity and equity derivatives, the tax law treats them legally as Equity Mutual Funds. For investors in the 30% or 39% income tax slab, this equity classification is a massive tax advantage over traditional fixed deposits.

How Liquid Funds Accrue Daily Interest

Liquid Funds do not touch the stock market or derivatives. Instead, they lend money to high-rated corporations, commercial banks, and the Government of India by purchasing short-term money market debt instruments. These include Treasury Bills (T-Bills), Commercial Papers (CPs), and Certificates of Deposit (CDs) with a maximum residual maturity of 91 days.

Because the maturity of these debt instruments is extremely short, Liquid Fund NAVs grow predictably through daily interest accrual (Yield-to-Maturity). They carry virtually zero interest rate duration risk because fluctuations in RBI repo rates have minimal impact on 90-day paper.

Taxation Breakdown: 30% Slab vs 20% STCG

Following recent debt mutual fund tax amendments, Liquid Funds are taxed strictly at your applicable income tax slab rate upon redemption. If you are in the 30% tax bracket, a 6.8% pre-tax Liquid Fund return nets you only 4.76% after tax.

In contrast, short-term capital gains (units held for less than 12 months) in Arbitrage Funds are taxed at a flat 20% STCG. A 7.0% pre-tax Arbitrage Fund return yields a net post-tax return of 5.60%—delivering over 84 basis points of pure tax alpha into your pocket.

When Should You Pick Liquid Funds Over Arbitrage?

Despite the tax advantage of Arbitrage Funds, Liquid Funds remain superior in three specific real-world scenarios:

  • Ultra-Short Parking (<15 Days): If you need your cash within 1 to 14 days, Arbitrage Funds charge a 0.25% exit load that erodes your short-term gain. Liquid Funds have zero exit load after Day 7.
  • Emergency Fund Buffer: Liquid Funds offer SEBI's Instant Redemption facility, allowing you to withdraw up to ₹50,000 (or 90% of folio value) instantly into your bank account 24x7 via IMPS.
  • Low Volatility Sideways Markets: When stock market trading volumes flatten out, cash-futures spreads compress. During prolonged flat phases, Liquid Fund interest rates can temporarily beat Arbitrage yields.

Practical Decision Blueprint

💡 Actionable Parking Rule

If you are in the 20% or 30% tax bracket and parking surplus money for 3 to 12 months (such as advance tax cash, annual bonus, or property sale proceeds awaiting reinvestment), park in Arbitrage Funds. If you are building an emergency safety fund or parking cash for less than 15 days, stick to Liquid Funds.

Practical Execution Nuances: Basis Risk, Rollover Spreads & Asset Allocation

While Arbitrage Funds offer compelling tax advantages for investors in higher tax brackets, executing an optimal cash management strategy requires understanding how these funds function across market cycles:

  • Basis Spread Fluctuations: When stock market volatility is high and retail participation surges, cash-futures spreads widen, pushing annual pre-tax arbitrage yields to 7.5% - 8.5%. During prolonged bear markets or low-volume consolidations, spreads compress toward 6.0% - 6.5%.
  • The Rollover Mechanism: On the last Thursday of each month (F&O expiry day), fund managers roll over expiring short futures positions into the next month. This captures the new basis spread without creating physical equity delivery friction.
  • Asset Allocation Role: Arbitrage funds are the ideal vehicle for parking short-to-medium term cash (3 to 12 months) like advance tax provisions, home renovation savings, or dry powder waiting to be deployed through an STP into our Step-Up SIP Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Arbitrage Funds completely risk-free?

Arbitrage funds are market-neutral because long cash equity positions are hedged with short futures positions. They carry near-zero market directional risk, though roll spreads fluctuate.

Why are Arbitrage Funds taxed like equity?

SEBI guidelines mandate that Arbitrage Funds hold over 65% exposure in equity and equity derivatives, qualifying them legally as equity funds for tax purposes.

Can Arbitrage Funds give negative returns?

In extremely rare low-volatility periods with negative roll spreads, monthly returns can flatten out, but annual returns historically remain positive.

When should I choose Liquid Funds over Arbitrage?

Choose Liquid Funds if you need guaranteed daily liquidity within 1 to 14 days, as Arbitrage Funds have 15-30 day exit loads.

Do Arbitrage Funds deduct TDS on redemptions?

No TDS is deducted on redemption for resident individual investors.
