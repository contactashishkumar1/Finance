No Cost EMI Truth: Hidden Interest Charges, GST & Foregone Cash Discounts

'Zero interest EMI' sounds like a free lunch, but RBI guidelines explicitly state that zero-interest loans do not exist. Discover the 3 hidden costs built into No-Cost EMIs.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

No-Cost EMI works by converting an upfront retailer discount into interest paid to the bank. Buyers lose the upfront cash discount and must pay an extra 18% GST on the monthly interest portion plus processing fees.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • RBI circular clarifies that interest is always charged by banks on EMI transactions.
  • Retailer discount is sacrificed to fund the interest payment to the credit card company.
  • You pay 18% GST on the interest component of every monthly EMI installment.
  • Banks charge a non-refundable EMI processing fee (typically ₹99 to ₹199 + GST).

How Repo-Rate Linked Home Loan EMIs Work in Practice

Modern Indian home loans are benchmarked directly to the RBI Repo Rate via Repo Rate External Benchmark (RLLR). When the Reserve Bank of India adjusts interest rates, commercial banks automatically adjust your loan interest rate, affecting either your monthly EMI payout or extending your total loan tenure.

Opting for partial annual principal prepayments (such as paying 1 extra EMI every year) drastically reduces your cumulative interest burden and cuts loan duration by up to 4 to 6 years.

Prepaying Principal vs Investing in Equity SIPs

If your home loan interest rate is 8.5% and expected equity SIP returns are 12%, investing surplus cash in equity SIPs yields higher long-term compounding. However, prepaying loan principal delivers a guaranteed, tax-free 8.5% return that provides immediate mental peace and balance sheet de-leveraging.

How E-Commerce No-Cost EMI Works Behind the Scenes

RBI regulations explicitly state that zero-interest loans do not exist. E-commerce platforms (Amazon, Flipkart) and banks structure No-Cost EMIs using two distinct accounting mechanisms:

  • Upfront Merchant Discount: The seller discounts the product price by the exact interest amount the bank will charge (e.g. ₹3,000 discount on a ₹50,000 phone). The bank then charges ₹3,000 interest over 6 months on the net ₹47,000 loan.
  • The 18% GST Loss: While the interest is subsidized by the merchant discount, the 18% GST charged on the monthly interest component is NOT discounted! You pay 18% GST on every monthly interest charge, plus a non-refundable one-time bank processing fee (₹199 + GST), making the loan costlier than paying upfront.
  • Forfeited Cash Discounts: Opting for No-Cost EMI frequently disqualifies you from instant 10% credit card upfront discounts, creating a major hidden opportunity cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is No-Cost EMI really zero interest?

No. The bank charges standard interest (14%-16% p.a.), but the seller gives an upfront discount equal to that interest amount. You still pay 18% GST on interest.

Why is 18% GST charged on No-Cost EMI?

Indian tax law treats financial interest paid to credit cards as a service, attracting 18% GST on the monthly interest component.

Can I cancel a credit card EMI after purchase?

Yes, you can request EMI pre-closure, but banks charge a pre-closure penalty (usually 2%-3%) and do not refund processing fees.

Does No-Cost EMI affect CIBIL score?

Timely EMI payments build your credit score positively. However, using a high percentage of your credit limit can temporarily lower your score.

Which option is best: Cash Discount vs No-Cost EMI?

Paying upfront cash with an instant discount is almost always cheaper than opting for a No-Cost EMI.
