Leave Encashment Rules for Resignation & Retirement

Have accumulated earned leave (EL / Privilege Leave) balance at your job? Discover how leave encashment payouts are calculated and taxed.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Leave encashment is a lump-sum amount paid by an employer for unavailed earned leave balance. While government employees enjoy 100% tax exemption, non-government employees get tax exemption up to ₹25,00,000.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Leave encashment received during service continuity is 100% fully taxable for all employees.
  • Leave encashment received at retirement or resignation has tax exemptions.
  • Government employees enjoy 100% tax-free leave encashment at retirement.
  • Non-government employees get tax exemption up to ₹25 Lakh statutory cap u/s 10(10AA).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum tax-free leave encashment limit?

The government enhanced the statutory tax exemption limit under Section 10(10AA) for non-government employees to ₹25,00,000.

Is leave encashment during service tax free?

No. Any leave encashment received while continuing employment with the company is 100% taxable as salary income.

How many earned leaves can be encashed per year of service?

As per tax rules, maximum 30 days of earned leave per completed year of service is permitted for calculating tax exemption.

How is 10-month average salary calculated?

It is the average basic salary plus DA received by the employee during the 10 months immediately preceding the date of retirement/resignation.

Does leave encashment attract Provident Fund (PF) deduction?

No. Leave encashment is not part of basic wages for PF calculation and does not attract EPF deductions.
