Inventory Turnover Ratio: Calculating Days Sales of Inventory (DSI)

Struggling with overstocked warehouses or stock-outs? Learn how to calculate Inventory Turnover Ratio to maximize retail and manufacturing profitability.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Inventory Turnover Ratio measures how many times a business sells and replaces its stock of goods over a given period, showing inventory efficiency and demand strength.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory.
  • Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) = 365 / Inventory Turnover Ratio.
  • High turnover indicates strong sales velocity and minimal holding costs.
  • Low turnover signals overstocking, obsolete inventory, or declining market demand.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good Inventory Turnover Ratio?

A ratio between 5.0x and 10.0x is generally healthy for most retail businesses, balancing product availability with low holding cost.

Why shouldn't inventory turnover be too high?

An extremely high turnover ratio may indicate inadequate safety stock, leading to frequent out-of-stock situations and lost sales.

How does inventory turnover affect cash flow?

Higher inventory turnover releases cash faster from inventory back into bank accounts, reducing reliance on expensive working capital loans.

Should revenue or COGS be used for inventory turnover?

COGS should always be used because inventory is recorded at cost, avoiding profit margin distortion.

What is deadstock?

Deadstock is inventory that has not sold for an extended period (typically >180 days) and is unlikely to sell without steep discounts or write-offs.
