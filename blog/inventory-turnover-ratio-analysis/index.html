Inventory Turnover Ratio: Calculating Days Sales of Inventory (DSI)

Struggling with overstocked warehouses or stock-outs? Learn how to calculate Inventory Turnover Ratio to maximize retail and manufacturing profitability.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Inventory Turnover Ratio measures how many times a business sells and replaces its stock of goods over a given period, showing inventory efficiency and demand strength.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory.
  • Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) = 365 / Inventory Turnover Ratio.
  • High turnover indicates strong sales velocity and minimal holding costs.
  • Low turnover signals overstocking, obsolete inventory, or declining market demand.

Accounting Principles, Adjustments & Regulatory Nuances

Evaluating Inventory Turnover Ratio requires delving deeper into financial statement analysis, accounting standards (Ind AS / IFRS), and corporate reporting quality:

  • Quality of Earnings & Cash Flow Validation: Reported net profits can be distorted by non-cash items, one-off asset sales, or aggressive revenue recognition policies. Comparing reported operating profit against actual Operating Cash Flow (OCF) reveals whether earnings are backed by hard cash collection.
  • Balance Sheet Health & Off-Balance Sheet Liabilities: Inspecting footnotes for contingent liabilities, pending tax litigation, corporate guarantees, and lease liabilities under Ind AS 116 ensures that solvency metrics reflect true financial obligations.
  • Working Capital Efficiency: Analyzing the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC = Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding - Days Payable Outstanding) shows how fast a company converts raw material investments back into cash in bank.

Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) Formula & Sector Benchmarks

The Inventory Turnover Ratio measures how many times a company sells and replaces its stock over a financial year:

Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory
Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) = 365 / Inventory Turnover Ratio
  • Sector Benchmarks: Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) and grocery retail (D-Mart, Trent) boast high turnover ratios of 8x to 15x (DSI of 25-45 days), enabling high returns on small margins. Luxury goods and heavy capital machinery operate at lower turnovers (2x - 3x / DSI 120-180 days) requiring higher gross profit margins.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good Inventory Turnover Ratio?

A ratio between 5.0x and 10.0x is generally healthy for most retail businesses, balancing product availability with low holding cost.

Why shouldn't inventory turnover be too high?

An extremely high turnover ratio may indicate inadequate safety stock, leading to frequent out-of-stock situations and lost sales.

How does inventory turnover affect cash flow?

Higher inventory turnover releases cash faster from inventory back into bank accounts, reducing reliance on expensive working capital loans.

Should revenue or COGS be used for inventory turnover?

COGS should always be used because inventory is recorded at cost, avoiding profit margin distortion.

What is deadstock?

Deadstock is inventory that has not sold for an extended period (typically >180 days) and is unlikely to sell without steep discounts or write-offs.
