NRE vs NRO Savings & FD Accounts: Taxability, Repatriation & Interest Math

Moving abroad or earning foreign income as an NRI? Learn the critical differences between NRE (Non-Resident External) and NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) bank accounts.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

NRE accounts hold foreign earnings transferred into rupees; interest earned is 100% tax-free in India and fully repatriable. NRO accounts manage income earned in India (rent, dividends); interest is subject to 30% TDS.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • NRE Account: Holds foreign income. Interest is 100% tax-free in India + 100% repatriable abroad.
  • NRO Account: Holds Indian income (rent, dividends, pension). Interest subject to 30.9% TDS in India.
  • NRO Repatriation Limit: Max $1,00,000 (1 Million USD) per financial year under RBI guidelines.
  • DTAA Relief: NRIs can lower NRO TDS from 30% to 10%-15% by utilizing Double Tax Avoidance Agreements.

NRE vs NRO Banking Architecture for Non-Resident Indians

NRIs moving abroad must convert existing Indian savings accounts into Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) accounts to legally manage domestic Indian income (rent, dividends, property sale). Foreign earnings remitted to India should be deposited in Non-Resident External (NRE) accounts.

Interest earned on NRE Savings and Fixed Deposit accounts is 100% tax-free in India under Section 10(4)(ii) and 100% freely repatriable back to your overseas country of residence.

Reducing NRO 30% TDS Using DTAA Tax Treaties

Interest earned on NRO deposits is subject to 30.9% TDS. However, NRIs residing in over 85 countries (including USA, UK, UAE, Canada) can submit Tax Residency Certificates (TRC) to claim Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) relief, reducing NRO TDS down to 10%-15%.

Statutory Legal Provisions & Regulatory Framework

Navigating NRE vs NRO Savings & FD Accounts in India requires a thorough understanding of the relevant Income Tax Act sections and regulatory guidelines established by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT):

  • Statutory Tax Exemption Provisions: Specific sections of the Income Tax Act govern allowances, deductions, and capital gain exemptions. Ensuring full compliance through proper documentation prevents tax audit notices and interest penalties under Section 234B/234C.
  • Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime Choice: Under Section 115BAC, individual taxpayers can evaluate tax liabilities under both regimes annually. While the New Tax Regime offers lower slab rates and a higher rebate under Section 87A, the Old Tax Regime remains advantageous for individuals claiming substantial HRA, Section 80C, 80D, and Home Loan interest deductions under Section 24(b).
  • Form 26AS & Annual Information Statement (AIS): Tax authorities automatically cross-verify salary Form 16, bank interest deposits, stock market capital gains, and high-value financial transactions against your AIS record.

NRE vs NRO Accounts: Taxability and Repatriation Rules

Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) must maintain designated bank accounts under FEMA guidelines:

  • NRE (Non-Resident External) Account: Maintained in INR for foreign earnings. Interest earned is 100% tax-free in India. Principal and interest are freely and fully repatriable abroad with zero limits or documentation.
  • NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account: Maintained for income originating in India (rent, dividends, pension, property sale). Interest is subject to 30% TDS (+ cess = 31.2%). Repatriation is capped at $1,000,000 USD per financial year, requiring Form 15CA/15CB chartered accountant certification.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an NRE Account?

An NRE (Non-Resident External) account is a rupee-denominated bank account opened by NRIs to park foreign earnings. Interest earned is completely exempt from Indian income tax.

What is an NRO Account?

An NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account is an Indian bank account opened by NRIs to manage income earned within India (such as rent, pension, or dividends).

Can a Resident Indian account be converted to NRE?

No. Existing resident Indian savings accounts must be converted into NRO accounts upon gaining NRI status.

Is interest earned on NRE accounts taxable in the NRI's home country?

While exempt from Indian tax, NRE interest may be taxable in your country of residence (e.g., USA, UK) depending on local tax laws.

How does DTAA reduce 30% NRO TDS?

Under Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) signed between India and over 85 countries, NRIs can submit Tax Residency Certificates (TRC) to reduce NRO TDS rates to 10%-15%.
