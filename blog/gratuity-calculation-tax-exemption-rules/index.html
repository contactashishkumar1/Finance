Gratuity Calculation & Eligibility Rules under Gratuity Act 1972

Completed 5 years of continuous service in a company? You are legally entitled to Gratuity. Learn how to calculate your lump-sum gratuity payout.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Gratuity is a statutory lump-sum financial benefit paid by employers to employees who have completed at least 5 years of continuous service. It is calculated using the 15/26 formula based on last drawn basic salary.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • 5 years of continuous service is mandatory to qualify for gratuity upon resignation or retirement.
  • The 5-year completion rule is waived in case of death or permanent disability of an employee.
  • Gratuity Formula = (Last Drawn Basic + DA) × (15/26) × Completed Years of Service.
  • Statutory tax exemption limit is ₹20 Lakh for covered non-government employees.

The 15/26 Gratuity Formula and 5-Year Eligibility Rule

Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, employees completing at least 5 years of continuous service are legally entitled to gratuity:

Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Basic + DA × Completed Tenure Years) / 26

Where 26 represents working days in a month, and 15 represents 15 days of salary per year of completed service. Fractions of a year exceeding 6 months are rounded up to the next full year (e.g. 7 years 8 months = 8 years).

  • ₹20 Lakh Lifetime Tax-Free Limit: Under Section 10(10), gratuity received by private sector employees covered under the Act is 100% tax-free up to a lifetime cumulative limit of ₹20,00,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 5 years mandatory for gratuity?

Yes, 5 years of continuous service with the same employer is mandatory. However, 4 years and 240 days (4 yrs 8 mos) is legally accepted as 5 years under court rulings.

Is gratuity deducted from monthly salary?

Gratuity is paid entirely by the employer. Some companies list it as part of your annual CTC package, but no monthly deduction is taken from your salary slip.

What is the maximum tax-free gratuity limit?

The government tax exemption cap on gratuity for non-government employees covered under the Act is ₹20,00,000.

What if I work 7 years and 4 months?

7 years and 4 months counts as 7 completed years of service (since 4 months is less than the 6-month rounding threshold).

Is gratuity payable if an employee is terminated?

Yes, gratuity is payable upon termination, except in cases of dismissal due to moral turpitude or willful damage to employer property.
