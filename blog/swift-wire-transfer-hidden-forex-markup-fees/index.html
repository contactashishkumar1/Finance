SWIFT International Wire Transfer: Hidden Forex Markup Rates & Intermediary Fees

Sending or receiving money internationally via bank wire? Learn how hidden exchange rate markups and intermediary SWIFT fees silently drain 3% to 6% of your transfer.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

A SWIFT wire transfer incurs three distinct fee layers: 1) Bank transfer fee (₹500-₹1,500), 2) Intermediary correspondent bank charges ($15-$30), and 3) Hidden exchange rate markup (2%-4% spread above mid-market rate).

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Forex Spread: Banks add a 2% to 4% markup on mid-market USD/INR exchange rates.
  • Correspondent Bank Deductions: 1 to 3 intermediary banks deduct $15 to $35 en route.
  • SWIFT Charge Codes: OUR (sender pays all), BEN (beneficiary pays all), SHA (shared).
  • TCS under LRS: 20% TCS applies to foreign remittances over ₹7 Lakh (excluding education/medical).

Cross-Border Legal & Regulatory Framework (RBI & DTAA Rules)

Executing international financial transactions in SWIFT International Wire Transfer requires strict adherence to Reserve Bank of India (RBI) regulations and international tax treaties:

  • RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS): LRS permits resident individuals to remit up to $250,000 USD per financial year for permissible capital and current account transactions. Transfers above ₹7 Lakh incur Tax Collected at Source (TCS) under Section 206C(1G).
  • Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA): India's tax treaties with over 85 countries allow resident and non-resident investors to claim Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) via Form 67, preventing double taxation on foreign dividends, interest, and capital gains.
  • FIRC & e-FIRA Compliance for Freelancers: Inbound foreign currency wire transfers require Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (e-FIRA) documentation to prove legitimate export of services and zero-rated GST status under Letter of Undertaking (LUT).

Forex Spread Optimization & Fee Minimization Strategies

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a SWIFT Wire Transfer?

SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) is a global messaging network used by financial institutions to securely send and receive international money transfers.

What is the difference between SWIFT OUR, BEN, and SHA charge codes?

OUR means sender pays all fees; BEN means recipient pays all fees from transferred funds; SHA means sender pays sending bank fee and recipient pays intermediary/receiving fees.

Why does the beneficiary receive less money than sent?

Because intermediary correspondent banks along the SWIFT routing chain deduct handling fees ($15-$30) directly from the transferred sum.

What is Mid-Market Exchange Rate?

The mid-market rate is the real midpoint exchange rate between buy and sell prices on global currency markets (the rate shown on Google/Reuters).

How does TCS on LRS foreign remittance work?

Under RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), outward remittances exceeding ₹7 Lakh per financial year attract 20% TCS (5% for education/medical loans). TCS can be claimed back when filing Income Tax Returns.
