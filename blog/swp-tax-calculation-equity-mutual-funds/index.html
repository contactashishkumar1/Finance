SWP Tax Calculation for Equity Mutual Funds

If you are generating a monthly income from Fixed Deposits, you are bleeding money to the tax department. A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) from equity mutual funds is mathematically the most tax-efficient way to generate a regular salary in India.

1. Why FDs are Terribly Inefficient for Monthly Income

Many retirees park their life savings in a Fixed Deposit (FD) and live off the monthly interest. The problem? FD interest is fully taxable at your applicable income tax slab rate.

If you are in the 30% tax bracket, generating ₹50,000 a month from an FD means almost ₹15,000 goes straight to the government. You only keep ₹35,000. This makes FDs highly inefficient for generating high-volume monthly income.

2. The Magic of SWP Taxation: Principal vs Gains

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) involves selling a small number of mutual fund units every month to generate cash. The secret to its tax efficiency is that you are only taxed on the profit component, not the entire withdrawal.

When you withdraw ₹50,000 via SWP, that ₹50,000 consists of two parts:

  • Principal: The original money you invested. (Tax-Free)
  • Capital Gains: The growth/profit on those specific units. (Taxable)

A Mathematical Example

Suppose you invested ₹50 Lakhs in an equity mutual fund. A few years later, the corpus grows to ₹1 Crore. The NAV (Net Asset Value) has exactly doubled. Therefore, every single unit you own is now 50% principal and 50% capital gain.

If you set up an SWP for ₹1,00,000 per month:

  • Withdrawal Amount: ₹1,00,000
  • Principal Portion (Tax-Free): ₹50,000
  • Capital Gains Portion (Taxable): ₹50,000

You are only liable to pay tax on ₹50,000, NOT the full ₹1 Lakh!

3. The First-In, First-Out (FIFO) Rule

How does the tax department know which units you sold? Indian taxation follows the FIFO (First-In, First-Out) rule.

The units you bought first are considered the units you sold first. If you started investing in the mutual fund 5 years ago, your SWP withdrawals today will sell those 5-year-old units. Because these units have been held for more than 12 months, the profits qualify as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG).

Important Note on Equity Taxation: If you withdraw units that you have held for less than 12 months, the profits are classified as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and are taxed at a flat rate of 20% (as per the latest budget). It is always recommended to start an SWP only from a corpus that is at least 1 year old.

4. The Ultimate Hack: The ₹1.25 Lakh LTCG Exemption

The Indian tax code offers a massive benefit for equity investors: The first ₹1.25 Lakhs of Long-Term Capital Gains in a financial year are completely tax-free. (Any LTCG above this limit is taxed at 12.5%).

Let's return to our previous example where you withdraw ₹1,00,000 per month. The capital gains portion of that monthly withdrawal was ₹50,000.

  • Total Annual Withdrawal: ₹12,00,000 (12 months × ₹1 Lakh)
  • Total Annual Capital Gains: ₹6,00,000
  • Tax-Free Exemption: ₹1,25,000
  • Net Taxable Gains: ₹4,75,000
  • LTCG Tax @ 12.5%: ₹59,375

You generated ₹12 Lakhs of cash flow, and you only paid ₹59,375 in tax (an effective tax rate of barely 4.9%). If you generated ₹12 Lakhs from FD interest in the 30% slab, you would have paid ₹3,60,000 in tax!

Conclusion

The mathematics of an SWP are undeniable. By leveraging the principal-return component and the ₹1.25 Lakh annual LTCG exemption, an SWP allows retirees and FIRE practitioners to draw a massive monthly income while paying virtually zero tax. The key is maintaining a diversified, low-cost equity and debt portfolio to sustain the withdrawals during market crashes.

Frequently asked questions

Is my entire SWP withdrawal amount taxable?

No. When you withdraw money via SWP, only the 'Capital Gains' portion of the withdrawal is taxed. The 'Principal' portion is your own invested money being returned to you, which is completely tax-free.

How does the ₹1.25 Lakh exemption apply to SWP?

In India, the first ₹1.25 Lakhs of Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) from equity mutual funds in a financial year are completely tax-free. If the capital gains portion of your total annual SWP withdrawals stays under this limit, your entire SWP income is practically tax-free.

What is the FIFO rule in mutual funds?

FIFO stands for First-In, First-Out. The Income Tax Department assumes that the mutual fund units you bought first are the ones being sold first during your SWP. This is crucial because it ensures your withdrawals qualify for Long-Term (12+ months) tax rates.

Is SWP payout taxed as dividend or capital gains?

SWP withdrawals are taxed strictly as capital gains (only the profit portion of redeemed units is taxed), making SWP vastly superior to taxable dividends.

How does the ₹1.25 Lakh annual LTCG exemption help SWP?

Long-term equity capital gains from SWP are 100% tax-free up to ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year.
