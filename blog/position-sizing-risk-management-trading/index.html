Position Sizing Math: Risking 1% Per Trade to Protect Capital

Poor position sizing is why 90% of retail traders blow up their accounts. Learn how professional traders use the 1% risk rule to survive losing streaks.

⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

Position sizing determines the exact number of shares or lots to buy based on total account capital, maximum allowable risk per trade (e.g., 1%), and distance to your technical stop-loss.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Never confuse trade allocation size with risk capital at risk.
  • The 1% Risk Rule: Never risk losing more than 1% of total trading account equity on a single trade.
  • Position Size = (Account Equity × Risk %) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss Price).
  • Proper position sizing ensures a 10-trade losing streak reduces capital by only ~9.5%.

The 1% Portfolio Risk Rule: The Secret to Long-Term Trading Survival

Professional traders never risk more than 1% to 2% of their total trading capital on a single trade:

Position Size (Shares) = (Total Capital × 1% Risk) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss Price)

If your account balance is ₹10,00,000, your maximum allowed loss per trade is ₹10,000 (1%). If you buy a stock at ₹1,000 with a stop loss at ₹950 (₹50 risk per share), your maximum position size is exactly 200 shares (₹10,000 / ₹50), regardless of how confident you feel about the setup!

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 1% risk rule in trading?

The 1% risk rule dictates that a trader should never risk more than 1% of their total trading portfolio balance on any single trade.

Is position size the same as total trade value?

No. Total trade value is the gross cash required to purchase shares. Position risk is the actual maximum loss incurred if your stop-loss is triggered.

How does stop-loss distance affect position size?

A tighter stop-loss allows a larger position size in shares for the same risk. A wider stop-loss requires a smaller position size.

What happens if a stock gaps down past my stop-loss?

Slippage can cause your trade to exit below your stop-loss. Professional traders keep overall leverage reasonable to absorb gap-down slippage.

What is Risk-to-Reward ratio (R:R)?

Risk-to-Reward ratio compares potential loss against expected profit target. A minimum 1:2 R:R ratio means risking ₹1,000 to make ₹2,000.
