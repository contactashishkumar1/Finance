⚡ Executive Summary

Quick Answer & Overview

A Protective Put involves buying an Out-of-the-Money (OTM) Put option on your underlying portfolio or index to cap maximum potential downside losses while leaving upside profit potential uncapped.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Acts as direct insurance against severe market crashes.
  • Caps maximum portfolio loss to a fixed percentage (e.g., 5%).
  • Maintains 100% uncapped participation in ongoing market rallies.
  • Cost of insurance equals the premium paid for the Put option.

The Cost of Portfolio Insurance: Direct Puts vs Collar Strategies

Buying OTM Index Put options acts as an insurance policy against severe market crashes (such as March 2020 or Global Financial Crises):

  • The Insurance Drag: Continuously rolling 3-month OTM Put options costs ~4% to 6% of your portfolio value annually in Theta decay, creating a noticeable drag on long-term returns during bull markets.
  • The Costless Collar Strategy: To eliminate hedging costs, combine a Long Protective Put with a Short Covered Call. The premium collected from selling the OTM Call fully finances the purchase of the Protective Put, creating a 100% free downside floor at the expense of capping extreme upside rallies.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Protective Put?

A Protective Put is a risk management strategy combining an owned stock/portfolio with a long Put option to limit downside losses.

How much does it cost to hedge a portfolio?

Hedging a portfolio typically costs 1% to 2% of portfolio value per quarter depending on market Implied Volatility (IV).

What is Beta-weighted hedging?

Beta-weighted hedging adjusts the number of index Put contracts purchased based on your portfolio's sensitivity (Beta) relative to the Nifty index.

Should I buy OTM or ITM Put options for hedging?

Out-of-the-Money (OTM) Put options (2%-4% below spot) offer the most cost-effective tail-risk insurance for retail portfolios.

When should I remove a protective put hedge?

Remove or roll the hedge once market volatility settles or when your technical indicators confirm a market bottom.
